# SGX3 Science Gateway Poster & Faculty Hackathon Blog

**Agbeli Ameko** · Howard University · FacultyHack@Gateways 2026

---

My experience started with attending a week-long virtual SGX3 Faculty Hackathon Series from August 3–14. The SGX3 Faculty Hackathon is a faculty workforce development initiative carefully programmed to support university faculty in integrating High-Performance Computing (HPC) and science gateway resources into their existing curricula. This appealed to me as I am a research faculty at Howard University’s Applied Data Science and Analytics program. At NSF NCAR’s Computational and Information Systems Laboratory I have worked in HPC and, in particular, with the NSF ACCESS Allocations program. I have participated in many hackathons and co-led seminars on utilizing sensors, open-source technologies, and computing at Howard and through programs such as SGCI.

SGX3 appealed to me as I am developing a new course at Howard called “Introduction to an Open Source Future: Decolonizing Technology & Data Sovereignty,” with the goal of introducing students to the world of Linux and open-source tools while doing data analysis. The course will also introduce students to the tools they can utilize to break free of big-tech monopoly and issues regarding data sovereignty. It is not only meant to be a compelling and spicy topic given the times we live in, but to provide real-world tools to empower students to function independently in this AI-driven workplace. The SGX3 Hackathon helped me integrate HPC into the course and learn from the diverse background of all of the other faculty members and my mentor, Dr. Mohamed Elbakary at ECSU, who brings years of HPC and classroom experience.

The SGX3 Faculty Hackathon does an outstanding job at addressing key challenges by providing dedicated time for me to focus on course design revision, offering technical mentorship from HPC experts, and fostering collaboration among educators from diverse institutions. I believe this has made my course better and will improve the student experience. For example, instead of simply relying on students to install one of two Linux distributions on their own hardware, we decided to leverage the NSF ACCESS Jetstream2 resource to provide students with accessible options. Further, through discussions with my mentor and other faculty cohort members, I have added datasets that support a compelling cross-cutting theme throughout the course that builds toward a final project.

This was my first time attending the Science Gateways conference, which was held in Washington, DC for its 10th anniversary session. It was a real pleasure to meet the other SGX3 Faculty Hackathon cohort members and sit down with my project mentor, Dr. Elbakary. I learned a lot speaking with other faculty about how they are utilizing AI and HPC in their classrooms to help improve my classroom offerings at Howard.

The in-person sessions built on the virtual collaborations we had established, with some very interesting sessions and group discussions. The sessions ranged from genomics, AI-ready workflows, and the NSF NAIRR pilot, to a dialogue with program officers regarding funding opportunities and proposals. The fishbowl discussion on AI was particularly fun and engaging as many people participated in the discussion, highlighting how comfortable the environment was for participants from all backgrounds. I did learn about a couple of new tools from attendees and speakers including Zellij, DesignSafe-CI, and Zo computer, which I am now testing out. An engaging and fun event that I look forward to attending again.
